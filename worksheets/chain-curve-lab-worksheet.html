Part A — Discover: The Catenary

A1. Reading the PhysicsDiscover mode

Click Reset chain so both pegs are back at their default position, and set Slack length = 30%, Gravity = 1.0. Let the chain settle.

Q1.Once the chain has settled, record the fitted catenary equation shown in the readout screen (values of a, h and k, to 2 d.p.).[2]
Q2.Predict what will happen to the chain's settling time — not its final shape — if you raise Gravity to 3.0. Test it, then describe what you actually observed.[2]
Q3.Set Gravity back to 1.0. Tick Add weight at centre link. Predict whether the catenary fit's RMSE will increase or decrease, then test it and record both RMSE values (before and after).[3]
Q4.Using the phrase "uniformly distributed load", explain why a chain with an extra weight at its centre no longer forms a true catenary.[3]
A2. Catenary vs ParabolaDiscover mode

Untick the centre weight. Tick both Catenary and Parabola under Fitted models.

Q5.Set Slack length to 5% (a shallow sag). Record the catenary RMSE and the parabola RMSE shown under the equations. Which model fits better?[2]
Q6.Now set Slack length to 100% (a deep sag). Record both RMSE values again. What happened to the gap between them?[2]
Q7.Using your answers to Q5 and Q6, explain in your own words why a parabola is often used as an approximation for a hanging cable, and when that approximation stops being good enough.[3]
Q8.Show by substitution that cosh(u) = (eu + e−u)/2 gives cosh(0) = 1. Then explain what x = h corresponds to physically on the chain, and why the equation should give its lowest point there.[3]

Part B — Challenge: Guess the Function

B1. Catenary targetChallenge mode

Switch to Challenge mode, target Catenary, and reset the chain to its default anchors and 30% slack.

Q9.Type a*cosh((x-h)/a)+k into the formula box and adjust a, h and k to push your Match score as close to 100% as you can. Record your final values and score.[4]
Q10.Click Reveal exact fit. Record the exact a, h, k values shown, then calculate the percentage error between your guessed a (Q9) and this exact a.[2]
B2. Parabola targetChallenge mode
Q11.Switch the target to Parabola (without changing the chain). Type a*x2+b*x+c and try to reach 100% match. Is 100% achievable here? Explain what your formula is actually being scored against — read the text that appears after clicking Reveal to check your answer.[3]
B3. Trying other functionsnot part of Maths Methods core content
Q12.Try a completely different family of function, e.g. sin(x)+cos(x)*2 or sqrt(abs(x)). What match score do you get, and why does it make sense that a trigonometric or square-root function is a poor model for a hanging chain's shape?[2]
